## 2. Findings (Discrepancies, Unchecked Steps, and §6 Inaccuracies)

### Severity: The claim is wrong (as literally stated in prose)

1. **Theorem 1(3), second sentence — missing scope of `SimilarlyOrdered`**
   - **Claim:** [answer_draft.md](file:///Users/andrew/maisi/questions/2026-11-20-odesky/answer_draft.md#L29): *"3. If the blocks are similarly ordered, then $c-d=\sum_iw_it_i|t_i|$ with $w_i:=\sigma_i^2/F$. Also $d=0$ if and only if every $t_i\ge0$, and $c=0$ if and only if every $t_i\le0$."* (See also §6 row 6, line 86.)
   - **Lean theorems:** `OrthoBlocks.main_d_eq_zero_iff` and `OrthoBlocks.main_c_eq_zero_iff` in [TheoremAExtra.lean](file:///Users/andrew/maisi/questions/2026-11-20-odesky/lean/OdeskyLean/TheoremAExtra.lean#L43-L62).
   - **What differs:** Both Lean theorems assume `(hso : SimilarlyOrdered B.α B.β)`. In Theorem 1(3), a period ends the first sentence, making *"Also $d=0$ if and only if every $t_i\ge0$, and $c=0$ if and only if every $t_i\le0$"* read as an unconditional statement on any sum of rank-one forms on orthogonal planes (and §6 line 86 does not mention `SimilarlyOrdered`). Without similar ordering, the forward implications are **false**: for $n=2$ blocks with $(\alpha_1, \beta_1) = (10, 1)$ ($t_1 = 9/11 > 0$) and $(\alpha_2, \beta_2) = (2, 5)$ ($t_2 = -3/7 < 0$), sorting gives $\lambda_+ = (10, 2)$ and $\lambda_- = (5, 1)$, so $\lambda_+ - \lambda_- = (5, 1) > 0$ and $d = 0$, despite $t_2 < 0$. (Symmetrically, $(\alpha_1, \beta_1) = (1, 10)$, $(\alpha_2, \beta_2) = (5, 2)$ gives $c = 0$ with $t_2 = 3/7 > 0$.)
   - **Seriousness:** **The claim is wrong** under the literal unscoped reading of the second sentence (harmless if rewritten so *"If the blocks are similarly ordered"* explicitly governs both sentences, matching `main_d_eq_zero_iff` and `main_c_eq_zero_iff`).

2. **§3, first special case — variance equality stated for arbitrary $f/g = (1+r)/(1-r)$**
   - **Claim:** [answer_draft.md](file:///Users/andrew/maisi/questions/2026-11-20-odesky/answer_draft.md#L49): *"for each entry, $(Q+K,\;K-Q)$ has the law of two independent centered Gaussians with variances $2(1+r)$ and $2(1-r)$, which is the law of the corresponding entries of $(2\sqrt f\,P,\;2\sqrt g\,M)$ for $f/g=(1+r)/(1-r)$."*
   - **Lean theorem:** `Odesky.correlated_gaussian_law` in [SpecialCases.lean](file:///Users/andrew/maisi/questions/2026-11-20-odesky/lean/OdeskyLean/SpecialCases.lean#L43-L48) (and docstring at [SpecialCases.lean](file:///Users/andrew/maisi/questions/2026-11-20-odesky/lean/OdeskyLean/SpecialCases.lean#L11-L13)).
   - **What differs:** Since $P_{ij}, M_{ij} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1)$ have unit variance, $(2\sqrt{f}\,P_{ij},\; 2\sqrt{g}\,M_{ij})$ has variances $(4f, 4g)$. These equal $(2(1+r), 2(1-r))$ **if and only if $f = (1+r)/2$ and $g = (1-r)/2$** (which is what the docstring in [SpecialCases.lean](file:///Users/andrew/maisi/questions/2026-11-20-odesky/lean/OdeskyLean/SpecialCases.lean#L13) states), not for general $f, g > 0$ satisfying $f/g = (1+r)/(1-r)$ (e.g., $f = 1+r,\, g = 1-r$ gives variances $4(1+r)$ and $4(1-r)$).    - **Seriousness:** **The claim is wrong** as literally stated in that clause (fixing $f=(1+r)/2, g=(1-r)/2$, or inserting *"up to a common scale factor"*, makes it true).
