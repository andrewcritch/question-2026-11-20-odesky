The formalization matches Theorem 1, the §1 identities and log-determinant, and the \(N\to\infty\) profile convergence in Theorem 2; the §3 claim that the question's correlated model has the same entry law as \((2\sqrt{f}\,P,\,2\sqrt{g}\,M)\) whenever \(f/g=(1+r)/(1-r)\) is false, and Lean does not check that identification.

I compared the claims in §1, §2, §3 and §7 with the theorem statements and the definitions they use (`GenProfile`, `Geometry`, `Profile`, `Rearrangement`, `Main`, `Limit`, `LogDet`). I did not recompile the project and I did not run `#print axioms`.

## Findings

### The correlated-model sentence is wrong, and stronger than its theorem

Claim, §3: for constant \(f,g\), the model \(K=rQ+\sqrt{1-r^2}\,G\) with \(|r|<1\) and standard Gaussian \(Q,G\) "is this case up to scale, entry by entry: for each entry, \((Q+K,\,K-Q)\) has the law of two independent centered Gaussians with variances \(2(1+r)\) and \(2(1-r)\), which is the law of the corresponding entries of \((2\sqrt{f}\,P,\,2\sqrt{g}\,M)\) for \(f/g=(1+r)/(1-r)\). In both ensembles, entries at different positions are independent."

Lean: `correlated_gaussian_law` in `SpecialCases.lean`. For \(|r|<1\) and independent standard normals \(X,Y\), with \(K=rX+\sqrt{1-r^2}\,Y\), the pushforward law of \((X+K,\,K-X)\) equals `gaussianReal 0 v₁` times `gaussianReal 0 v₂` whenever \(v_1=2(1+r)\) and \(v_2=2(1-r)\).

What differs:

- The one-entry law of the question's model, including independence of the two coordinates and the variances \(2(1\pm r)\), matches the theorem. §6 states this half, and that half is accurate.
- The "which is" clause is false. `Var(2\sqrt{f}\,Z)=4f` for a standard normal \(Z\). The ratio \(f/g=(1+r)/(1-r)\) does not fix \(4f\). At \(f=1+r\), \(g=1-r\) the ratio matches and \(4f=4(1+r)\neq 2(1+r)\). The comment in `SpecialCases.lean` has the scale that does work, \(f=(1+r)/2\), \(g=(1-r)/2\), and the answer drops it. The ensemble actually used in Theorem 2 divides by \(\sqrt{N}\), so its entries \((Q+K,\,K-Q)\) have variances \(4f/N\) and \(4g/N\). The displayed comparison is to \((2\sqrt{f}\,P,\,2\sqrt{g}\,M)\) without that factor.
- "Up to scale" can repair the profile law, because the profile is invariant under a common scale of \(Q\) and \(K\). It does not make the stated equality of entry laws true for every pair with that ratio. The variance check \(4\cdot(1+r)/2=2(1+r)\) is not in the Lean theorem.
- Independence of entries at different positions is not a statement of `correlated_gaussian_law`, which is one pair \((X,Y)\). For the ensemble it is the i.i.d. hypothesis of `theoremB_ae`. For the question's model it is unchecked.

Seriousness: the equality of laws at a mere ratio is wrong. The ensemble identification, including cross-position independence, is unchecked as stated. §6 does not make this false claim.

## Claims that match

The claim I checked hardest is the general-profile reading of \(\lambda_+\), \(\lambda_-\) and \(\sigma(T)\), because a wrong convention on sorted eigenvalues would make relation (1) and the log-determinant a different statement. It matches the question. In `GenProfile.lean`, `eigS` and `eigT2` are Mathlib's decreasing eigenvalues of \(S\) and of \(T^\top T=-T^2\) (`roots_charpoly_eq_eigenvalues` in `Weyl.lean` identifies those with characteristic-polynomial roots). `lamP j` is the \(j\)-th decreasing eigenvalue of \(S\), cut at \(0\); `lamM j` is the absolute value of the eigenvalue in position \(N-1-j\), cut at \(0\); `sigT j` is the square root of the eigenvalue of \(T^\top T\) in position \(2j\). Under `rank F=2n`, spelled `LinearIndependent` of `stackRows q k` together with \(2n\le N\), `lists_pos`, `eigS_pos` and `eigS_neg` put \(n\) positive eigenvalues of \(S\) first and \(n\) negative ones last, and `pair_eq` makes the positive eigenvalues of \(T^\top T\) equal in consecutive pairs. So `sigT` is the list of \(n\) singular values counted once, decreasing, and `lamP`, `lamM` are \(\lambda_+\) and \(\lambda_-\). `frobL` and `frobT` are the squared Frobenius norms (`frobL_eq`, `frobT_eq`). `gA,gB,gC,gD` are \(a,b,c,d\), and `gErr` is the relation-(1) relative error of §4.

§1, first bullet. For orthonormal frames with the same \(W\), `exists_isometry_of_same_W` supplies the orthogonal map of frames, and `gprof_isometry` / `gprof_eq_of_same_W` give the same \((a,b,c,d)\) and the same `gErr`. `lists_isometry` gives the same three lists. Same \(L\) implies the same profile and lists by `gprof_of_formL_eq`. The answer's \(N\ge 2n\) is stronger than these theorems, which do not use it.

§1, second bullet. `m2` and `qrec` in `Identities.lean` are the answer's \(m_2\) and \(q\). `gA_identity` is \(a=(1-m_2-q)/2\) for \(\sigma_i>0\) and orthonormal \(u,v\), \(n\ge 1\). `gprof_sum_eq_one` is \(a+b+c+d=1\) when also \(2n\le N\). `gA_sub_gB_identity` is \(a-b=c+d-m_2-q\). The question's standing hypothesis \(n\le N/2\) is the extra hypothesis on the second identity.

§1, third bullet. `logdet_identity` is \(\sum_j\log\sigma(T)_j=\frac12\sum_j\log(\lambda_{+,j}\lambda_{-,j})\) for \(2n\le N\) and rank \(F=2n\). `prod_sig_eq_det` and `prod_lam_eq_det` are \(\prod_k\sigma(T)_k^2=\prod_k\lambda_{+,k}\lambda_{-,k}=\det G/4^n\), with `gramRows` the Gram matrix of the stacked rows, the answer's \(G=FF^\top\). The prose form \(\frac12\log\det G-n\log 2\) is that product after taking logarithms: \(\frac12\log(4^n)=n\log 2\). The mean of \(r_i=\log\sigma(T)_i-\frac12\log(\lambda_{+,i}\lambda_{-,i})\) is then \(0\).

§2, definitions. `OrthoBlocks` is \(L=\sum_i s_i u_i v_i^\top\) with \(s_i>0\), unit \(u_i,v_i\), and \(\langle u_i,u_j\rangle=\langle u_i,v_j\rangle=\langle v_i,v_j\rangle=0\) for \(i\neq j\), so \(W\) is diagonal. `t`, `α`, `β` are the answer's tilt and block eigenvalues. `SimilarlyOrdered` is \((\alpha_i-\alpha_j)(\beta_i-\beta_j)\ge 0\). For unit vectors, \(u_i\neq\pm v_i\) is \(|t_i|<1\). Normal form is that condition plus one sign of all tilts. \(n\) orthogonal 2-planes force \(N\ge 2n\); the Lean hypothesis `2 * n ≤ N` does not narrow the claim.

§2, Lemma 1. `S_add` and `S_sub` in `Geometry.lean` are the eigenvector equations \(S(u_i+v_i)=\alpha_i(u_i+v_i)\) and \(S(u_i-v_i)=-\beta_i(u_i-v_i)\). `charpoly_S` is \(\prod_i(X-\alpha_i)(X+\beta_i)\,X^{N-2n}\). `roots_S_pos` and `roots_S_neg` say the positive eigenvalues are the \(\alpha_i\) and the negative ones are the \(-\beta_i\). `α_pos` and `β_pos` give the signs. `neg_T_sq_add` and `neg_T_sq_sub` give eigenvalue \(\alpha_i\beta_i\) of \(-T^2\) on the whole plane, and `charpoly_neg_T_sq` is \(\prod_i(X-\alpha_i\beta_i)^2\,X^{N-2n}\), so the singular value \(\sqrt{\alpha_i\beta_i}\) has multiplicity 2.

§2, Theorem 1(1)–(4). For \(n\ge 1\), \(|t_i|<1\), \(2n\le N\), and `SpectralLists` the decreasing lists cut out of the characteristic polynomial (`spectralLists_exists` builds them), the operator profile `(pa,pb,pc,pd)` is the question's \((a,b,c,d)\):

- `main_a_le_b` and `main_a_eq_b_iff`: \(a\le b\), with equality if and only if the blocks are similarly ordered.
- `main_relation_one`: similarly ordered implies \(\sigma(T)_k=\sqrt{\lambda_{+,k}\lambda_{-,k}}\) at every \(k\).
- `main_c_sub_d`: \(c-d=\sum_i(s_i^2/\|L\|^2)\,t_i|t_i|\), and \(\|L\|^2=\sum_i s_i^2\).
- `main_d_eq_zero_iff` and `main_c_eq_zero_iff`: under similar ordering, \(d=0\) if and only if every \(t_i\ge 0\), and \(c=0\) if and only if every \(t_i\le 0\).
- `main_mem_Theta_iff`: \(a=b\) and \(cd=0\) if and only if the blocks are similarly ordered and the tilts are all \(\ge 0\) or all \(\le 0\). That is \(\pi(L)\in\Theta\) if and only if \(L\) is in normal form, inside this class.

§2, Theorem 1(5). `theta_attained` takes \(a>0\), \(c,d\ge 0\), \(cd=0\), \(2a+c+d=1\) and returns a one-block `OrthoBlocks 1 2` in normal form whose profile is \((a,a,c,d)\). Those equations are \(\Theta\) inside the tetrahedron \(a+b+c+d=1\), \(a,b,c,d\ge 0\), and \(a>0\) drops the endpoints \((0,0,1,0)\) and \((0,0,0,1)\).

§3, Theorem 2, convergence. The ensemble's rows are `qOf` and `kOf`: row \(i\) of \(Q\) is \(\sqrt{f_i}\,x_i-\sqrt{g_i}\,x_{n+i}\) and row \(i\) of \(K\) is \(\sqrt{f_i}\,x_i+\sqrt{g_i}\,x_{n+i}\), with \(x\) the rows of \([P;M]/\sqrt{N}\). That is the displayed formula for \(Q+K\) and \(K-Q\). `theoremB_ae` fixes \(n\ge 1\) and fixes positive vectors \(f(\rho),g(\rho)\), and for i.i.d. standard Gaussian columns, almost surely \((a,b,c,d)\) tends to `(profA, profB, profC, profD)` of those vectors and, when the two vectors are similarly ordered, `gErr` tends to \(0\). Conditioning on \(\rho\) and sending \(N\to\infty\) with \(n\) fixed is this quantifier order. `blocks_of_limit_gram` turns a realization of the limit Gram into an `OrthoBlocks` with \(\alpha_i=f(\rho_i)\), \(\beta_i=g(\rho_i)\) and \(|t_i|<1\). `gprof_at_limit` identifies the general profile of any such realization, for \(2n\le N\), with that formula profile. The orthonormal realization used in the proof is `limit_config`.

§3, consequences. `similarlyOrdered_of_monotone`: global nondecreasing \(f,g\) make every sample \((f(\rho_i),g(\rho_i))\) similarly ordered, so the blocks of \(L_\infty\) are similarly ordered and the relation-(1) error tends to \(0\). `limit_a_eq_b`: the limit profile has \(a=b\), so \(a-b\to 0\). `limit_mem_Theta`: if also \(f-g\) is everywhere \(\ge 0\) or everywhere \(\le 0\), the limit profile lies on \(\Theta\). The Lean sign hypothesis is the answer's "does not change sign on \(\mathbb{R}\)". These three theorems are about the formula profile; with `blocks_of_limit_gram` the same \(\alpha,\beta\) are the block eigenvalues of an orthogonal-plane form, which is the normal-form condition.

§3, the \(n\to\infty\) limit. `cd_eq_ratio` is \(c(L_\infty)-d(L_\infty)=\sum_i(f-g)|f-g|(\rho_i)\big/\sum_i(f+g)(\rho_i)^2\), which is \(\sum_i w_i t_i|t_i|\) with \(t=(f-g)/(f+g)\) and \(w\propto(f+g)^2\). `cd_limit_ae` sends this to \(\mathbb{E}[(f-g)|f-g|]\big/\mathbb{E}[(f+g)^2]\) almost surely as the number of blocks tends to infinity, for i.i.d. \(\rho\), measurable nondecreasing \(f,g>0\), and \(\mathbb{E}[(f+g)^2]<\infty\). Nondecreasing functions are measurable, so that hypothesis does not narrow the claim. The numerator is integrable once the denominator is, because \(f,g>0\) gives \(|f-g|\le f+g\). The right-hand side is the \((f+g)^2\)-weighted mean of \(t|t|\). The answer's "if also" puts this under the one-sign hypothesis; Lean does not need one sign. The proved statement covers the claim.

§3, Gaussian baseline. For \(f=g=c>0\), `const_profile` gives the formula profile \(\bigl(\tfrac12,\tfrac12,0,0\bigr)\) at every index permutation, and `theoremB_ae` is the convergence. Constant equal coordinates are antitone, so the sorting hypotheses hold. This is the midpoint of \(\Theta\).

§7. `theoremB_ae` fixes \(n\) and sends \(N\to\infty\), so it does not cover \(N=\kappa n\) with \(n\to\infty\). The measurements and the fixed-ratio formula are outside the theorem list, as the answer says. `fixed_ratio_bound` is the algebraic bound named in the caveat.

§6's other rows name the theorems that prove the claims above. The axiom sentence and the absence of `sorry` are not re-checked here.
