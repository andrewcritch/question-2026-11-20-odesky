**Theorem 2: quantifiers and limits.**

[TheoremBFull.lean:560, theoremB_ae](/Users/andrew/maisi/questions/2026-11-20-odesky/lean/OdeskyLean/TheoremBFull.lean:560) fixes positive vectors `fv,gv : Fin n → ℝ` before its almost-everywhere quantifier. Its randomness is the column sequence `Z`. The columns are measurable, pairwise independent and identically distributed; the coordinates of the first column are mutually independent standard Gaussians. These hypotheses follow from the answer's stronger i.i.d.-entry specification. They do not demand extra moments of the latent strengths.

`colVec` takes the first `N` columns of the same sequence and divides by `sqrt N`. `qOf` is `sqrt(fv)*P/sqrt N − sqrt(gv)*M/sqrt N`; `kOf` uses the plus sign. Thus `Q+K` and `K−Q` have exactly the factors of two in line 40. The theorem concerns four coordinate limits and, under similar ordering, convergence of `gErr` to zero.

This order of quantifiers agrees with “Condition on ρ”: substitute the fixed values `fvᵢ=f(ρᵢ)`, `gvᵢ=g(ρᵢ)`. There is no formal conditional probability kernel or joint random variable `(ρ,P,M)` in this theorem. That is a harmless representation of the stated conditional claim; it would not by itself be a theorem about joint almost-sure convergence with random strengths. In particular, I did not silently interchange `∀ strengths, almost surely` with `almost surely, ∀ strengths`.
